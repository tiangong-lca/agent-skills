# Guide evidence collection

Use this guidance when starting material intake or helping the user fill an evidence gap. Work in the response language selected at the start of the Skill. The outcome is a usable source inventory and a concrete next request, not a completed questionnaire. This responsibility can be delegated with the goal, available files and provisional scope.

## Give the user a useful starting point

Read what the user has already said or supplied. If needed, establish what product/service is being assessed and what decision the study should support, in business language. Offer a provisional interpretation of scope rather than asking the user to define LCA terminology. Do not ask them to repeat readable facts.

Translate the actual product and likely operations into a short prioritized request. Usually begin with two or three useful record types, not the entire lifecycle checklist. For each, say what it will establish and suggest a likely source within the business. Accept spreadsheets, bills, PDFs, photos, screenshots, emails and informal notes as maintained by the enterprise. If upload is unavailable, accept pasted relevant rows or a description; no mandatory folder or workbook format is needed.

Choose from the following according to scope and what is already available. This is an internal guide, not a questionnaire to send wholesale.

| Need | Familiar records to suggest | Likely place to ask | What they establish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Product and materials | Product specification, BOM, recipe, material issue/return records, packaging list | Product team, engineering, purchasing or warehouse | Product composition, quantities and packaging; distinguish design quantities from actual consumption. |
| Operations and output | Process sketch, work order, batch report, good/reject counts, outsourcing order | Production planner, workshop or subcontractor | Which operations occur where, corresponding output, losses and shared production. |
| Purchased energy and water | Electricity/fuel/steam/water bills, meter readings, equipment run logs | Facilities, workshop or finance | Consumption for a stated meter, site and period; pair with output for the same boundary. |
| Transport | Delivery note, waybill, shipment weight, supplier origin and destination | Purchasing, warehouse or logistics | Shipment amount, route and mode; defer distance estimates until endpoints are known. |
| Waste and direct releases | Scrap sales records, waste transfer notes, treatment records, monitoring reports | Workshop or environmental staff | Amount, treatment route and measured releases; a blank is not zero. |
| Use and end of life, when in scope | Care instructions, service life information, customer-use evidence and disposal arrangements | Product team, after-sales or customer | Supported usage/disposal scenarios and where assumptions need a decision. |

For a cotton T-shirt, a first request might prioritize a product specification or material list, a recent batch's fabric issue/output record, and the corresponding workshop energy record. Ask where knitting/dyeing/sewing occur only as needed to locate the relevant evidence. Product-specific suggestions do not establish those operations or quantities as facts.

## Make the request easy to act on

A natural Chinese opening after the user names a T-shirt could be:

> 可以先从现有资料开始，不用整理成标准模板。先发一份产品规格或物料表，以及最近一批的领料、产量记录，能找到哪份就先发哪份。我会先识别每件衣服用了什么、对应哪批生产；接下来再对照同一时期的能耗。如果这些不在你手上，可以先找产品/采购同事要规格或物料表，找车间要领料和完工记录。截图或照片也可以。

English equivalent, shown only for an English conversation:

> Start with the records you already have; no special template is needed. Send a product specification or material list and, if available, the material issue and output records for a recent batch. I’ll use these to identify the materials per shirt and the production batch, then match its energy use. The product or purchasing team may have the specification; the workshop may have the batch records. Screenshots or photos are fine.

Adapt the opening to the actual case and existing answers. Do not send both language examples or every table row. If the study's intended use is still unknown, add one relevant plain-language question rather than a separate technical intake form.

## Close the loop after each batch

Acknowledge which files were read and summarize what they establish, what remains uncertain and the next useful item to obtain. Keep a simple working list of received, still-needed and unavailable material. Request the missing denominator with the quantity: for example, after reading a monthly electricity bill, ask for that site's output in the same month and whether the meter also covers other products. Explain why the answer matters; do not merely ask for “more data.”

When a record is unavailable, suggest a practical alternative: a warehouse movement record instead of a BOM, a supplier specification, an existing meter log, or a measurement the user can arrange. Ask what the user can actually access. Label estimates and proposed assumptions distinctly; adopt them only with an explicit basis. Missing critical quantities retain a specific user handoff, while extraction of other files continues. If neither a record nor a justified substitute is available, state the affected calculation and the concrete next collection action.

Retain original filenames and private source locations, page/sheet/section, original units and reporting period, and owner/confidentiality information when supplied. Do not burden the user with filling metadata that can be extracted. Keep private originals in the authorized workspace; supplying files is not permission to publish them. If content is unreadable, identify the affected page or cells and ask for the smallest useful alternative. Pass readable materials and unresolved questions to [evidence extraction](evidence-extraction.md) without waiting for the entire collection to be complete.
